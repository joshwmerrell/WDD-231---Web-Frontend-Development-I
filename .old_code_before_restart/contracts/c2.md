# Learning Contract: Semantic and Accessible HTML

Student: Joshua Merrell  
Date: October 6, 2026  
Target assessment date: October 6, 2026

## Primary competency

By the end of this contract, I can build and explain a clear, semantic page structure for my spiritual blog that uses meaningful HTML elements, logical headings, accessible navigation, and well-formed content sections. I can identify when a page is relying on visual styling instead of structure and fix it in a way that supports keyboard users and screen-reader users.

## My starting point

I already know/can:

I already have a site at https://sonofgod.jmerrell.online/ and I can update the HTML for the pages I publish. I understand that the project is turning into a spiritual blog and that the page structure needs to be clearer and more intentional as the content grows.

I still need to learn or improve:

I need to improve how consistently I choose semantic elements such as header, nav, main, section, article, and footer. I also want to become more deliberate about heading hierarchy, landmark structure, and accessible names so the page is easier to use with a keyboard and more understandable to assistive technology.

## My learning plan

I will use these resources:

- [WDD 231 Competency Guide](https://byui-cse.github.io/wdd231-course/resources/competencies/)
- [Learning Contract instructions](https://byui-cse.github.io/wdd231-course/ponder/learning-contract/)
- [MDN: HTML elements reference](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Element)
- [WebAIM: Keyboard Accessibility](https://webaim.org/techniques/keyboard/)

I will practice by:

I will review the current page structure for the spiritual blog, identify the main content regions, and rebuild the page shell so the header, navigation, content, and footer are each clear and intentionally structured. I will check that the heading flow makes sense, that landmarks are used appropriately, and that any interactive controls have clear names and labels.

My first milestone, due October 8, 2026, is:

I will revise the main page of the spiritual blog to use semantic HTML structure, a clear heading hierarchy, and accessible navigation, then I will record a quick keyboard walkthrough showing the focus order and page regions.

## Evidence of learning

I will submit or demonstrate:

- The updated blog page with semantic structure and clear content regions.
- A short explanation of why the page uses certain elements and how that improves meaning and accessibility.
- A keyboard-only walkthrough showing that the important links and controls can be reached in a logical order.
- One structure or accessibility issue I identified, how I diagnosed it, and what I changed.

I will know I am ready when:

- I can explain the difference between a styled layout and a semantic page structure.
- I can identify the major page regions and justify my heading and navigation choices.
- I can complete a keyboard-only check and explain what I would fix if a section of the page were not accessible.

## Support and risks

The help I may need is:

I may ask my instructor or a peer to review my structure and explain whether my heading and land-mark choices are clear and logical.

A risk to my plan is:

The site content may continue to evolve while I am refining the structure, so I will keep the milestone focused on one main page and the core accessibility improvements rather than trying to redesign the entire site at once.

## AI use, if applicable

I expect to use AI for:

I may ask AI for examples of semantic HTML patterns or quick checks for accessibility issues, but I will keep the task focused on my own page and not let AI decide the structure for me.

I will verify AI suggestions by:

I will compare them to the course guidance and MDN/WebAIM examples, test the behavior with the keyboard and browser tools, and explain the final structure in my own words before I rely on it.
